# Submission: Northstar Projects Spend Analytics

Net spend 1 Jan to 30 Sep 2026: INR 4.56 Cr across 869 transactions (910 records; 4 duplicates, 22 declined and 15 reversed excluded).

## Top three insights

**1. Payments are being completed without a valid approval.**
149 transactions worth INR 88.2 L show as completed while approval is pending (105 transactions, INR 68.9 L) or rejected (44 transactions, INR 19.3 L). The approval step is not acting as a gate, and a rejected payment that still completes suggests an override or no system enforcement. A further 101 approvals have been pending for over 30 days, and 56 transactions have no receipt with no policy exception recorded.

**2. One contractor drives a quarter of spend, and September more than tripled its run-rate.**
SiteServe Contractors received INR 1.12 Cr, 24.6% of net spend. In September it took INR 35.5 L, 46% of that month's spend, and its median payment rose from INR 80 k in January to INR 3.2 L. Only 11% of its spend went through the Vendor Payment route; the rest moved by UPI, reimbursement or card. The largest single payment, INR 8.2 L (T000109, Hyderabad Site, UPI), still has approval pending.

**3. Duplicate invoices and payments split just under 50,000.**
Four records (T000901 to T000904) repeat the invoice ID, employee, vendor and amount of earlier transactions (INR 1.9 L potential double payment). Diya Sharma (Operations) made six UPI payments to Metro Hardware of 49,750 or 49,800, always two on the same day (14 Jul, 22 Aug, 8 Sep), INR 3.0 L in total, all approved by the same approver and compliant on their own. This pattern suggests splitting to stay under an approval limit; the 50,000 limit is an assumption to confirm.

Also: Finance spent INR 43.1 L against a INR 17.2 L budget (251%). Operations, Procurement and Projects used only 13% of INR 23.1 Cr budgeted, so those budgets need clarifying before utilisation is relied on.

## One thing to investigate further

The SiteServe Contractors payments in September, and why most contractor payments bypass accounts payable. Before concluding anything I would want: the contract and work orders (rates, scope, any September scope increase or catch-up), invoices plus measurement or attendance records for each September payment, the payee name and account behind each UPI transfer, whether the approver of record was authorised for these amounts and why the largest items were released while pending, and whether employees paid the contractor personally and were reimbursed.
